Legal Framework & Political Dimension
[IMG]
Map of Engagement Network
The legal framework and political dimension of genetic engineering are often closely intertwined. Throughout our project, we conducted several interviews where we talked about both, especially regarding upcoming EU regulations on plants produced using New Genomic Techniques NGTs. Here, we would like to give an overview of the entirety of interviews we conducted that helped us understand the impact of patents on real-world applications and how our project can be brought into alignment with the political dimension.
In order to get an overview of the legal basis and the situation in patent law regarding plants generated via NGTs, we participated in the webinar hosted by the GASB: GASB meets PAW – Genetic Engineering Key Issue #3: Patents.
There we learned about the eco-progressive NGO EcoProg and connected with Prof. Natalie Laibach and Dr. Robert Hoffie, members of the steering committee, who helped us contextualize our project within the NGT framework of the upcoming EU regulations. When talking about the patent aspects within the NGT framework, we learned that the new EU law aims to prevent patents on NGT-modified plants, which could facilitate easier development and commercialization of such plants without the need for costly licenses.
Seeking further legal guidance when it comes to plants being generated through NGTs, we consulted Prof. Hans-Georg Dederer, an expert in constitutional and European law. While giving us insights into the mutual influences between patents and political decisions, he also helped us understand and contextualize the corresponding consequences for our project. He further advised us to engage with the patent offices at our university, which led to productive discussions with Vanessa Kuhl and Dr. Claudia Nitsch from the administration team for research contracts of the Philipps-University Marburg. They clarified the university's rules on patents and the distinctions between open-source and open-license frameworks, which are important for our project's goal to facilitate accessibility outside of the academic context.
To deepen our understanding of patent law in general after the GASB webinar, we reached out to Matthias Hoffmann, a European Patent Attorney, who provided essential perspectives on navigating the patent landscape. In academia, patents often seem distant, but when working on projects with real-world applications, understanding and navigating patent landscapes becomes crucial.
In order to explore the broader impact of patents on biotechnology and to learn from the experiences of other projects, we reached out to multiple people.
Therefore, we consulted Dr. Richard Jefferson, founder of Cambia and The Lens. His expertise offered us a valuable perspective on the complex world of patents and their significant role in shaping real-world applications, especially in the field of biology.
Similarly, our conversation with Dr. Ingo Potrykus, co-developer of Golden Rice, underscored how biotechnology can address global issues while managing patents in a way that maintains accessibility, something we aim to replicate in our project.
With our interview with Dr. Dheeraj Rathore, a research expert in non-Agrobacterium-mediated transformation (non-AMT), we wanted to broaden our understanding of alternatives for AMT and why he sees the necessity for such. His expertise greatly informed our approach to non-AMT strategies, and we're grateful for his contribution to refining our methods.
