## 1. Project Overview
[IMG]
This project addresses a major global public health crisis in fungal infections by developing a synthetic biology–based therapeutic platform utilizing the Type VI Secretion System (T6SS). The engineered bacterial therapy specifically targets the WHO-designated “critical priority” fungal pathogens, including Candida auris and Aspergillus fumigatus.
The project aims to overcome the limitations of current antifungal drugs—high resistance, severe toxicity, and limited R&D pipelines—and to provide a highly efficient, low-toxicity, and programmable therapeutic solution for fungal infections.
Business Model
The project adopts a “dual-track model of innovative drug development and licensing collaboration”, operating under a capital-light strategy with a core objective of accelerating global market entry.
R&D and Regulatory Strategy
The regulatory pathway focuses on efficient approval channels in both the U.S. and China:
- United States: Leverage the FDA’s Accelerated Approval Pathway for live biotherapeutic products (with potential exemption from Phase III trials), reducing the time to market by 2–3 years.
- China: Utilize the “Conditional Approval” policy (such as the Boao real-world data pilot program), where real-world studies can substitute parts of traditional clinical trials, enabling faster domestic market access.
Licensing and Partnership Strategy
To mitigate financial and R&D risks, the project implements a License-out cooperation model:
- Out-license late-stage clinical development and commercialization rights to major pharmaceutical partners with established global distribution networks.
- Secure milestone payments and sales-based royalties (with partnership intentions already signed with BGI Genomics and Bioin Biotech), ensuring early cash flow realization.
Market Strategy
A “North America First, China Next” dual-engine market strategy will be adopted:
- North America as the primary launch market: With a transparent regulatory framework and strong payment capacity (U.S. antifungal drug market projected at USD 4.7 billion in 2024), FDA approval will be prioritized.
- China as the secondary market: Leverage the Hainan Medical Special Zone policy to accelerate domestic product rollout and reimbursement integration, achieving synergistic international market linkage.
Financing Plan
The financing roadmap is tightly aligned with R&D milestones and proceeds in three rounds:
- Angel Round (RMB 80 million): Supports strain optimization and preclinical toxicology studies.
- Pre-A Round (RMB 50 million): Initiates Phase I clinical trials (20–100 participants, safety validation).
- Series A Round (RMB 80 million): Advances Phase II proof-of-concept clinical studies.
Funding allocation prioritizes R&D investment (70%), with the remaining 30% dedicated to team development and regulatory compliance, ensuring optimal resource concentration toward core technology translation.
Strategic Summary
Through the combined leverage of policy advantages, capital acceleration, and strategic licensing, this project establishes a high-efficiency pathway from laboratory innovation to global commercialization—driving the T6SS-based antifungal therapy toward becoming the next-generation breakthrough in infectious disease treatment.
## 2. Project Background
2.1 Burden of Fungal Diseases
• Massive death toll: Approximately 3.8 million people die from fungal diseases globally each year (Denning, Lancet Infect Dis 2024), surpassing malaria (0.61 million in 2022) and tuberculosis (1.4 million).
• Causal attribution gap: >60% of fungal-related deaths in low-income countries remain undercoded (GAFFI 2023), leading to systematic underestimation of the true burden.
• Disease spectrum: Invasive fungal diseases (IFD) cause 6.5 million annual cases with a 58.5% fatality rate; superficial fungal infections affect 1.3 billion people (WHO 2023), ranking third in prevalence after dental caries and tension-type headache.
2.2 Pathogen Spectrum
Among the over 600 fungal species capable of infecting humans, only 30 cause routine diseases. These pathogens have co-evolved thermotolerance, meaning they can grow at 37°C.
Three dominant pathogens prevail in clinical infections:
- Candida species: Account for 40% of bloodstream infections, with Candida albicans being the most common pathogen. Drug-resistant Candida auris strains have spread to 50 countries, posing an increasingly severe resistance challenge.

- Aspergillus species: Account for 70% of pulmonary infections. Aspergillus fumigatus is responsible for 90% of invasive aspergillosis cases, and in Asia, its resistance rate to azole antifungals has reached 20%.
[IMG] f2
- Trichophyton species: Account for 80% of skin infections, with the global prevalence of onychomycosis rising to 15% (according to JAMA Dermatology 2023 data).
[IMG] f3
These figures indicate that the global prevalence and drug resistance of these three pathogens—particularly their resistance to conventional antifungal medications—have become significant public health challenges.
From the perspective of infection classification and clinical threat, invasive infections are highly lethal.

2.3 Treatment Bottlenecks
- Drug Resistance: Candida auris has been elevated to an “emergency threat” level by the U.S. CDC, with multidrug resistance rates reaching 42% in East Asia. This directly narrows the clinical accessibility window for the two mainstay antifungal agents, echinocandin and azole drugs. Under sustained selection pressure from agricultural triazole pesticides, Aspergillus fumigatus resistance rates in Europe have risen to 20%, concurrently increasing treatment failure rates for standard itraconazole/voriconazole regimens and creating urgent demand for high-cost second-line oral and inhaled formulations.
- Depletion of Therapeutic Tools: Systemic antifungal drugs remain confined to four major classes—polyenes, azoles, echinocandins, and pyrimidine analogues. Only three new molecular entities have been approved over the past 20 years, with pipeline scarcity far exceeding that of antibiotics. Existing agents exhibit pronounced dose-limiting toxicities: amphotericin B causes nephrotoxicity in 49% of patients, while flucytosine induces myelosuppression in 25%. These limitations restrict optimal dosing, prolong hospital stays, and increase dialysis costs, creating significant premium potential for less toxic novel therapies.
- Diagnostic Gap: Only 12% of laboratories in low- and middle-income countries possess mycological testing capabilities (GAFFI 2023). Non-culture diagnostics (β-D-glucan, GM, PCR, MALDI-TOF) suffer from low penetration rates, contributing to 58.5% of invasive fungal disease deaths attributable to delayed diagnosis or treatment failure. Once rapid POCT and companion diagnostics are included in medical insurance or centralized procurement, they can drive volume growth before drug market launch, creating a high-barrier “water seller” market segment.
2.4 Global Policy Responses
In October 2022, the WHO released its inaugural Fungal Priority Pathogens List (FPPL), marking the first inclusion of fungal infections within the global antimicrobial resistance (AMR) governance framework. The list explicitly identifies four categories of “extremely high priority” pathogens: Candida auris (multidrug-resistant), Aspergillus fumigatus (azole-resistant), Candida albicans (fluconazole-resistant), and Cryptococcus neoformans (highly lethal CNS infections). The core significance of this list for the pharmaceutical industry lies in its ability to leverage policy to accelerate the concentration of capital and R&D resources toward these highly lethal, highly resistant fungal indications. This effect mirrors the valuation boost the 2017 WHO Priority Bacteria List provided for antibiotic R&D. It offers new impetus for advancing research in these areas and may profoundly impact the treatment of fungal infections.
Therefore, fungal diseases have become a global public health crisis on par with malaria and tuberculosis, yet systemic gaps persist in areas such as mortality attribution, new drug development pipelines, and diagnostic accessibility. Over the next five years, novel antifungal drugs—particularly those targeting the Gwt1, Hsp90, and Cdr1 pathways—combined with parallel breakthroughs in rapid point-of-care molecular diagnostics, will be key to reducing the annual death toll of 3.8 million.
## 3. Pain Points of Existing Drugs
3.1 Drug Development
For decades, clinical practice has relied solely on four major classes of drugs (polyenoids, azoles, pyrimidines, and echinocandins), spanning over 60 years. From 2001 to 2023, only three novel mechanism drugs were launched (Ibrexafungerp, Olorofim, Rezafungin), while the WHO warns that 90% of drugs in development remain optimizations of existing targets. Only four classes of systemic antifungal drugs are clinically available, with a weak pipeline for new drug development.
3.2 Drug Defects
- Polyene (1949–)
- Representative: AmB-d
- Critical drawback: Renal toxicity occurs in 49% of cases, driven by high-affinity binding of the drug to cholesterol in renal tubular epithelial cell membranes, subsequently activating the mitochondrial apoptosis pathway. Clinical data show approximately 30% of patients experience doubled creatinine levels, with another 30% facing dialysis risk.
- Clinical outcome: Forced treatment duration shortening and dose reduction directly elevate treatment failure rates by 15–20%.
[IMG] f5
- Azoles (1969–)
- Representatives: Fluconazole, Voriconazole
- Critical Drawback: Triazole drugs significantly increase drug interaction risks through broad-spectrum inhibition of the CYP450 enzyme system. For example, voriconazole can increase tacrolimus C_max by approximately 3-fold. Since the triazole skeleton itself cannot circumvent this flaw, its use in transplant and ICU settings is significantly restricted.
- Flucytosine Analogs (1968–)
- Representative: Flucytosine
- Critical Drawback: Within 72 hours of monotherapy, FCY2 gene mutations rapidly drive up resistance rates. Clinically, forced combination therapy with amphotericin B (AmB) is often required. However, this regimen compounds bone marrow suppression (mediated by human cytidine deaminase), resulting in discontinuation rates of approximately 10–15% among patients.
- Echinocandin (2001–)
- Representative: Candida
- Fatal Limitation: Zero activity against Cryptococcus and Mucorales, resulting in significant gaps in antimicrobial spectrum; lipopeptide structure precludes oral administration, restricting use to intravenous delivery in ICUs with limited accessibility.

Breakthrough in U.S. FDA Regulations
In June 2025, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) formally released the pivotal document “Streamlined Approval Pathways for Living Biological Therapy Products,” sparking widespread attention in the medical field. This policy aims to provide a more efficient approval process for synthetic biology-based topical therapeutic agents, accelerating the market entry of innovative therapies. According to the document, topical therapeutic formulations meeting specific criteria may be exempted from Phase III clinical trials. These criteria include: addressing unmet clinical needs with a fatality rate exceeding 30%, achieving an animal model cure rate higher than 80%, and demonstrating a biological escape rate below 10⁻⁷.
This policy provides a more streamlined pathway to market for synthetic biology-based innovative drugs, significantly shortening the R&D cycle—particularly for fatal diseases lacking effective treatments. This shift not only accelerates the dissemination of novel therapeutics but also grants relevant enterprises a competitive edge in global markets, facilitating the widespread adoption of novel treatment approaches.

#### 5.1.3 Society Analysis
Deficiencies in Primary Care Diagnostic Capabilities
According to the 2025 report released by the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention, the misdiagnosis rate for fungal infections in township clinics has risen to 63.5% (95% CI: 60.1–66.9%). The root cause of this issue lies in the widespread lack of essential rapid diagnostic equipment at primary healthcare facilities, with an equipment availability rate below 12%. Due to the scarcity of diagnostic tools, primary healthcare workers often rely on clinical experience to assess fungal infections, leading to difficulties in early diagnosis and frequent misdiagnosis.
This situation directly fuels the overuse of broad-spectrum antibiotics, with an abuse rate as high as 84.7%, according to the China Primary Healthcare Blue Book 2025. The excessive use of broad-spectrum antibiotics fails to effectively combat fungal infections while disrupting the host's microbial communities. This significantly increases selection pressure for drug-resistant bacteria, further exacerbating the resistance problem.
The potential harm of this vicious cycle to public health systems cannot be overlooked. Frequent misdiagnosis and mistreatment accelerate the evolution of resistant bacteria, resulting in a selection coefficient as high as 0.21. This indicates significantly enhanced adaptive capabilities of resistant bacteria in survival competition. According to a 2025 study in Nature Ecology & Evolution, the detection rate of resistance genes in Candida auris strains prevalent in primary care settings increases by as much as 18.3% annually. As an increasingly severe drug-resistant pathogen in recent years, Candida auris—particularly its rapid spread and expanding resistance in primary care settings—has become a major threat to public health security.
The heightened resistance of Candida auris not only poses therapeutic challenges but also complicates infection control, presenting significant public health challenges that gravely endanger public health and safety. To address this escalating crisis, there is an urgent need to promote more advanced fungal infection diagnostic technologies in primary healthcare facilities, restrict the misuse of broad-spectrum antibiotics, establish scientifically sound antibiotic stewardship policies, and increase investment in monitoring and controlling drug-resistant bacteria.
Drug Accessibility
According to The Lancet's 2025 report, approximately 3.2 billion people worldwide face drug shortages. Findings indicate that the root cause lies not solely in poverty, but in inadequate global healthcare infrastructure—particularly the lack of effective pharmaceutical supply chains and supporting facilities—which constitute the primary constraints on drug accessibility.
When delving into drug accessibility, the “Drug Accessibility Iceberg Model” provides a precise analytical framework to reveal its complexity and underlying causes. The visible portion of the model (approximately 10%) manifests as readily observable drug shortages and patients' inability to access essential medicines. While these phenomena are partially linked to poverty, they represent only the surface layer of the problem, falling far short of its core. The hidden portion of the iceberg (approximately 90%) constitutes the deep-seated causes of drug accessibility issues. These concealed factors include supply chain disruptions, particularly in low-income countries and remote regions.

These technological advantages enable engineered bacterial gels to not only effectively overcome existing infrastructure limitations but also provide an innovative pathway to address global drug accessibility challenges. By reducing dependence on traditional cold chain transportation, lowering production costs, and enhancing transport efficiency, engineered bacterial gels hold promise for improving the sustainability and equity of pharmaceutical supply chains worldwide. This advancement will promote widespread drug accessibility in resource-constrained regions, ultimately contributing to enhanced global public health and the realization of health equity.

8.1 MVP Design
MVP (Minimum Viable Product) refers to a product prototype developed in the early stages of product development that satisfies user needs with the most fundamental and core features. The core concept of MVP is to launch a product with minimal functionality early on—sufficient to validate market demand—testing product hypotheses at the lowest cost and in the shortest time possible. This approach facilitates rapid collection of user feedback, identifies product pain points and opportunities, and enables iteration and optimization.
The key advantage of an MVP lies in its ability to prevent overdevelopment and resource waste, ensuring teams can validate market demand and user response before full product launch. Through an MVP, companies can identify potential issues early, avoiding excessive investment in features that fail to meet market needs. Additionally, an MVP enables low-risk market entry, allowing for gradual refinement of product functionality and increased success rates. Consequently, it plays a crucial role in entrepreneurship and product development, particularly in high-uncertainty market environments.

## 10.Risk Analysis and Response Strategies
10.1 Risk Interview
[IMG] f53
Through an in-depth conversation with Mr. Zhang Kewei, Partner at Beijing Fanhai Consulting Management Co., Ltd. (FA), we gained a more systematic understanding of **risk management**, **particularly in quantifying risks,** **addressing capital shortages, and developing contingency plans**. Mr. Zhang pointed out that modern enterprises face a wide array of risks, especially in technology-driven innovative companies, where effectively identifying and addressing these risks has become critical to success.
First, Mr. Zhang emphasized the quantification of risks. Traditionally, risk management has often focused on qualitative analysis, but in modern corporate operations, quantifying risks has become particularly important. By quantifying different types of risks, the impact level, probability of occurrence, and financial consequences of potential risks can be made concrete, thereby providing decision-makers with more accurate basis for decision-making. Mr. Zhang noted that constructing risk assessment models and scenario analyses can quantify risks such as cash flow disruptions, delayed technology development, and intensified market competition, while predicting the specific losses these risks may incur.
Addressing funding shortages, Mr. Zhang specifically highlighted their prevalence among startups and innovative enterprises. Such shortages not only risk halting R&D progress but also disrupt daily operations and market expansion plans. Mr. Zhang recommended that enterprises develop detailed capital management plans and pursue diverse funding channels, including venture capital, equity financing, and bank loans. He also advised maintaining sufficient cash reserves to withstand sudden financial pressures. Communication and collaboration with investors are crucial, particularly during funding crunches. Maintaining open dialogue with capital partners and promptly adjusting financing strategies can help businesses navigate cash shortages.
Mr. Zhang Kewei further emphasized the importance of contingency planning. Throughout a company's lifecycle, encountering various external and internal risks is inevitable, making thorough contingency preparation a vital safeguard for long-term development. He noted that risk contingency plans should cover everything from basic emergency responses to complex recovery strategies, including addressing risks such as cash flow disruptions, technological failures, and market contraction. A robust risk contingency plan not only enables swift and effective responses to crises but also enhances a company's adaptability and competitiveness in the face of uncertainty. Mr. Zhang shared several successful case studies from his professional experience, where companies successfully navigated periods of capital shortages and market turbulence by establishing emergency management mechanisms and disaster recovery protocols.
Overall, our discussion with Mr. Zhang Kewe revealed that risk management transcends mere loss prevention—it serves as a strategic tool enabling sustained corporate growth amid uncertainty. Enterprises should quantify risks, optimize capital management, and develop comprehensive contingency plans to ensure steady progress through diverse challenges, thereby safeguarding long-term healthy development.
10.2 Risk Identification and Assessment
To identify key risks, the business model will prioritize evaluating potential hazards. Specifically, each risk will be assessed across three dimensions—impact, probability, and uncontrollability—using a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (low) to 5 (high). The risk index is calculated by multiplying these three factors, and risks are categorized based on this index. A high risk index indicates a critical risk requiring effective monitoring and management, while a low risk index signifies a risk that only requires monitoring.
