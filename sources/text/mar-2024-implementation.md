Implementation
[IMG]
Map of Engagement Network
When we started our research, we didn't fully understand the practical challenges of bringing a new crop like Taraxacum kok-saghyz into real-world farming. These issues, often overlooked in literature, became clear only after consulting experts in the field. We therefore talked to stakeholders in the fields of agriculture and plant breeding:
Dr. Fred Eickmeyer, head of ESKUSA company, emphasized the limitations of breeding solely for rubber content, as plants with high rubber levels often fail to thrive in the field. His work on crossbreeding and improving seed harvesting has been instrumental in shaping our approach. Johannes Frank, a dandelion farmer, gave us an important inside view into the work with dandelions. Sebastian Rhiel, a local farmer, highlighted practical concerns, such as root morphology, financial risks, and the need for specialized equipment. Jana Gäbert's input as a farmer stressed the complexities of integrating new crops and the importance of comprehensive information and support.
In order to help overcome a few of these struggles, we developed a MoClo (Modular Cloning) toolbox to facilitate genetic modifications in dandelions, focusing on traits such as root depth, seed size, and flowering time. To identify key endogenous genetic parts, we conducted transcriptomic analyses. Additionally, we created a Dandelion Handbook aimed at supporting both farmers and researchers by providing essential guidelines and insights for working with this crop, ensuring that our research has real-world applicability.
While our initial focus was on rubber yield, these discussions highlighted other key factors like seed handling, biomass, and field performance. These are practical challenges that we hadn't anticipated in the lab. Engaging directly with those who would use our product made us realize how important it is to talk to stakeholders early in the process.
We learned that successful implementation requires input from many perspectives. Even if a plant is engineered perfectly, other challenges – such as financial risks – can arise.
The process is complex, involving much more than just the technical side of crop development. Besides these practical challenges, we therefore also explored the legal framework and political dimension that influence implementation and talked to economic stakeholders, ensuring our approach also covers the views in these fields.
