# GOAL:
Synthetic biology requires sustained attention and human resources for continuous development. In line with our research, we have organized a variety of educational activities aimed at engaging individuals from diverse backgrounds in the field of synthetic biology. These initiatives seek to involve a broader audience to shape, participate in, and comprehend synthetic biology through our events. We have designed lectures covering various topics, from fundamental principles to real-world applications, customized to our target participants. Additionally, we have organized diverse activities to involve our participants, fostering their interest and understanding of synthetic biology. With this educational endeavor, we invite you to join us on an exciting journey to make synthetic biology education more accessible, enjoyable, and interactive.
# Children
## Why did we reach out to them?
Children are full of potential, just like "pluripotent stem cells." Learning about synthetic biology early will spark children's interest and pave the way for future advances in the field. Through these educational activities, we'd like to introduce basic biology knowledge to children and allow them to participate in several activities. Our goal is to enhance children's and their parent's interest in biology by providing them with fun and attractive lectures and activities.
## What did we do?
## 1. Biology Workshop at the Hong Kong Children Charity Foundation
[IMG]
We organized a biology workshop called "Adventure to the Field of Biology" on 12 and 15 August 2024 in collaboration with the Hong Kong Children Charity Foundation (HKCCF). A group of children aged 4-6 joined our workshop with their parents. The sessions were conducted in English, Mandarin Chinese, and Cantonese Chinese to facilitate inclusive learning for all attendees.
We introduced them to genetics and cells during two days of workshops. We began our workshop by asking several questions, such as, "Which part of you do you think looks like your parents?", "if the mother flower has a pink color and the father flower has a white color, then which color will the baby flower be?". These short questions enabled us to form a friendly and engaging environment, making kids participate more actively. After the lecture, we prepared DNA origami folding and play dough to craft animal cells to provide kids with fun and intriguing activities.
Our workshop was characterized by active participation from both children and parents. Children and parents asked several questions during and after the workshop. One of the memorable questions from parents was "SRY misplacement in X chromosome," which stemmed from our lecture on genetics. We were grateful to answer questions from our participants and see them enjoying our workshop.
## 2. Synthetic Biology Activity Handbook
[IMG]
In an extension of preparing the workshop collaborated with the HKCCF, we realized that these synthetic biology workshops are barely organized in local communities. Hence, we created a handbook with activities and instructions for event organizers to promote synthetic biology education in local communities and schools worldwide.
The handbook includes several activities customized to the workshop's target participants, which range from kindergarten to secondary school students. For example, we suggest the arts and crafts of common biology-related concepts, such as DNA and cells, for kindergarten children, as we previously did in our workshop.
To extend the target readers of our handbook, we translated it into multiple languages, including Russian, Korean, Indonesian, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese. We also provided a list of materials and detailed procedures for the activities to lessen the burden on event organizers. By easing the preparation for organizing the synthetic biology workshop, we hope students from diverse age groups get interested in learning synthetic biology.

# High School Students
## Why did we reach out to them?
The high school curriculum generally underlines fundamental and theoretical knowledge; hence, synthetic biology is not widely known among high school students. As young scientists, we have devised a plan to organize synthetic biology activities aimed at high school students across the globe.
Our goal is to spark their interest in the field of biology, particularly synthetic biology, and provide them with an understanding of how biology can contribute to creating a better world. We believe that event will enable them to expand their knowledge and contribute to solving real-world issues.
## What did we do?
## 1. Synthetic Biology Summer Camp for High School Students (Collaboration with Nazarbayev University iGEM Team)
[IMG]
CityU iGEM team 2024 joined Nazarbayev University iGEM team’s synthetic biology summer camp for high school students across Kazakhstan. Along with the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology (HKUST) iGEM team, we were honored to be the guest speakers of the event, delivering synthetic biology presentations to 179 students participating online. On 15 July 2024, we shared our expertise in Synthetic DNA, Aptamers, Affinity Bioanalysis, and Lateral Flow Assays (LFA) through a fun and interactive presentation. At the end of the session, students participated in an insightful Q&A, and we hoped that our answers addressed their curiosity. We also hoped that the students would be able to apply their new knowledge in their future studies or research in genetic engineering.
## 2. Online Synthetic Biology Talk, “Biohacking for a Better World”
[IMG]
CityU iGEM team 2024 organized an online synthetic biology talk named "Biohacking for a Better World" on 29 July 2024. We invited five universities worldwide, including the City University of Hong Kong, to share their topic in this online seminar.
Each university shared its astonishing journey to bring its synthetic biology knowledge and enthusiasm to make a better world. Here, we’d like to briefly introduce excellent teams that joined our talk and their topics:
- The Chinese University of Hong Kong's Talk: "What is synthetic biology?"
- The City University of Hong Kong's Talk: "How can aptamers be used as an express test to detect counterfeit anti-cancer drug, Bevacizumab?"
- Nazarbayev University of Kazahkstan's Talk: "Novel bacteriotherapy methods for cancer treatment"
- Shen Zhen University's Talk: "Modern transgenic technology and its application in production"
- Vienna University's Talk: "Waste water bioremediation, how can laccases break down pharmaceuticals?"
In this online synthetic biology talk, our team gave a concise talk about our project: “How can aptamers be used as an express test to detect counterfeit anti-cancer drug, Bevacizumab?” We shared our step-by-step process of how we came up with our current topic and the significance of the testing kit for counterfeit anti-cancer reagents. During the talk, we presented our background knowledge of biosensors, including their types, principles, and applications. Furthermore, we displayed the schematic design of our testing kit for a clear understanding of our participants.
As a part of our syn-bio talk, we prepared Kahoot games to enhance the engagement of our participants. These games were arranged between and after our syn-bio talk to assess their apprehension of the projects and strengthen their background knowledge of the significance of synthetic biology.
We have successfully concluded our online synthetic biology talk with high school participants from eleven countries. An outstanding 70% of the participants rated our Synthetic Biology Talk the highest score, with 85% responding that they gained new insights and knowledge from the talks.
“I learn a lot of new topics about synthetic biology and genetic engineering. It is so cool to see how synthetic biology could be applied to address real-world problems. ## 3. Biosensors Educational Video (Collaboration with Washington University iGEM Team)
As a part of our project, we made an interview video introducing our novel testing kit for detecting counterfeit drugs titled: "Aptamer-Based Biosensors: Revolutionizing Diagnostic and Sensing Technologies" in collaboration with Washington University iGEM Team.
This video starts with an introduction to the importance of biosensors in modern society, highlighting their application in human healthcare and the environment. It also covers various types of biosensors, such as antibody-based, nucleic acid biosensors, enzymatic biosensors, etc. Moving on, this video delves into detailed explanations of aptamer-based biosensors and how we leverage this technology to combat counterfeit drugs.
Unlike the previous workshop we organized, this video includes a more precise description of our initiatives and the technical implementation of our projects, which have been elaborated in our LFA subpage. We believe that describing our project through video enables efficient delivery of our project overall.
The interview video showcases our innovative testing kit, making it a valuable resource for high school students to gain insights into the intersection of science and technology.Aptamer is not a familiar topic among high school students, hence we began with providing the background of aptamer in the biosensor field. By investigating the role of biosensors in healthcare and environmental applications, students develop a better knowledge of real-world issues and advancements. The film not only presents several types of biosensors, but also highlights cutting-edge aptamer-based technology, encouraging critical thinking about how science might handle urgent concerns such as counterfeit pharmaceuticals. Furthermore, this multimedia method improves communication skills and fosters teamwork, making complicated scientific topics more understandable and entertaining. Overall, this activity provides students with knowledge and skills necessary for their future academic and professional endeavors.

## 4. Synthetic Biology Workshop at Cognitio College, Hong Kong
[IMG]
We organised a workshop called “Bio-builders: Know more about Synthetic Biology and DNA” on 23 August 2024 in collaboration with Cognitio College. A group of secondary high school biology students joined our workshop. Our workshop was conducted in Cantonese and English to facilitate inclusive learning for all attendees and to have a better explanation of several contents.
We introduced our project topic during our workshop: “Investigate an express test for bevacizumab.” Also, we introduced some DSE (Hong Kong Diploma of Secondary Education Examination） concepts, such as the structure of DNA and RNA, base-pairing rules, and the process of DNA replication. This session can allow students to get insight into the difficult concept and manage it in the following DSE exam. Furthermore, we introduced some drug actions, such as intercalating agents and alkylating agents. We introduced its chemical reaction and the results. Those contents can allow students to know more about why drugs can heal different diseases and can spark more interest in biology. We also experimented with making DNA from bananas. This experiment can allow students to know more about what DNA looks like. Finally, we made a kahoot quiz for students to know how much they learned. The teacher said, “This workshop is well prepared, and the students enjoy it a lot”.
After the workshop, we have distributed our recently made mascot toy to students to promote our project in their community.

## 5. Synthetic Biology Workshop at United Christian College Kowloon East (UCCKE), Hong Kong
[IMG]
We organized a workshop at United Christian College Kowloon East (UCCKE) called ”Bio-Builders: Designing the Future with Synthetic Biology”, on 11 September 2024. This workshop allowed students to gain a deeper understanding of how synthetic biology can be leveraged to address real world challenges. Collaborating with the UCCKE iGEM team, we introduced the iGEM competition and our synthetic biology research projects. Thus, students had the chance to connect with the passionate researchers behind the innovative projects and share their own perspectives.
Furthermore, after the research projects presentation and basic synthetic biology introductions, the students got to do their own experiments, both in-person and virtually. With the help of UCCKE’s iGEM team, the students had the opportunity to do a wet-lab activity which is gel electrophoresis. Students learned first-hand on how the technique could separate mixtures of DNA, RNA, or proteins according to molecular size. In addition to the wet-lab experiment, the students were guided to learn about CRISPR-Cas9 mechanisms and applications through an interactive website, at labxchange.org. Through labxchange.org, students were also assisted to do laboratory simulations such as column chromatography protein purification, gel electrophoresis of the TAS2R38 gene, and pH scale demonstration. Students were able to get deeper insights on other synthetic biology applications.

# University Students & General Public
## Why did we reach out to them?
Undergraduate students have a strong background in biology and may have various opportunities to explore the field of synthetic biology, such as organizing projects. Therefore, in educational programs designed for undergraduate students, we primarily focused on conveying our messages with professional knowledge and sharing our experience in organizing projects. Yet, since most online materials focus on expertise in the field, we attempted to convert those materials into a more readable and understandable format for undergraduate students. Doing so, we aim to pave the way for more university students to learn and participate actively in synthetic biology.
## What did we do?
## 1. IHP Human-Centered Design Handbook
[IMG]
Human practices account for one of the most important parts of developing projects. These involve, but are not limited to, expert advice and gathering stakeholder needs. However, human practices can easily be disregarded during the project development. Accordingly, we created an Integrated Human Practice (IHP) handbook to highlight the significance of human practices and share our experience dealing with them.
Our partnership to build the IHP guide derives from a shared awareness of how critical human behaviors are to the success of any project. By combining our different knowledge and experiences, we want to create a comprehensive resource that highlights the significance of engaging stakeholders and gives practical ways for effectively incorporating their thoughts throughout the project's lifetime. This effort is critical because it promotes a culture of inclusion and responsiveness, which leads to more imaginative and effective solutions. This guidebook has far-reaching implications; it enables project teams to emphasize human-centered methods, improves stakeholder satisfaction, and raises the chance of project success by ensuring that all perspectives are heard and respected in decision-making. This initiative to create the IHP handbook is invaluable for university students as it provides them with valuable skills and information for future employment. By highlighting the significance of human behaviors in project creation, students learn the value of cooperation, stakeholder participation, and ethical considerations—skills that are sometimes disregarded in typical academic environments.

## 2. Bioethics Handbook (Collaboration with Queen’s University iGEM Team)
[IMG]
Besides applying knowledge and understanding the core problems, there are various things to consider while developing the project. Ethics, one of the core values of human beings, needs to be thought through thoroughly during the project. Hence, we created an Ethics Handbook for undergraduate students in collaboration with Queen's University, the University of Florida, Arizona State University, and Stanford University.
Our work with Queen's University to establish the Ethics Handbook for undergraduate students is essential in encouraging a moral attitude to academic and professional endeavours. This guidebook not only defines essential ethical concepts but also offers practical advice on overcoming challenging moral quandaries that students may face during their projects. We hope to foster an academic culture of honesty and responsibility by giving students the tools to examine ethical issues critically. This effort has a tremendous impact because it enables future leaders to make educated, ethical decisions that benefit society, ensuring that their work not only fulfils academic requirements but also corresponds with larger ideals such as respect, justice, and social responsibility.
The handbook tackles theoretical implications and applications of ethical considerations. It incorporates real-life applications and case studies such as usage, production, distribution, and pricing of our LFA based on all the concepts and ethical considerations mentioned in the ethical handbook.
## 3. BIOME Picture Book (Collaboration with McGill University iGEM Team)
[IMG]
We are collaborating with the McGill University team to create a BIOME picture book focused on RNA-DNA-related proteins. The BIOME Picture Book is an engaging and instructive photo collection depicting the vast world of synthetic biology. This collection aims to clarify complex scientific concepts for a broader audience, particularly younger learners. Each page features vibrant graphics and insightful captions that introduce readers to the exciting world of synthetic biology, showcasing topics from genetically engineered organisms to revolutionary biotechnologies.
We aim to clarify complicated scientific ideas for a wider audience, particularly younger learners, by using attractive graphics to describe protein interactions with DNA and RNA. This program is critical because it not only encourages interest and comprehension of molecular biology but also underlines the role of visual learning in scientific education. The picture book's impact goes beyond mere illustration; it is a valuable resource that encourages students to explore the molecular world, sparking a passion for science and potentially inspiring the next generation of researchers and innovators in genetics and molecular biology.
