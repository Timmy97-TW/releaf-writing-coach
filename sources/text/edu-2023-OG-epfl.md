The magnitude of change achieved by scientists and engineers has always been related to its support in society. The lack of understanding by society’s members can cause those ideas to fall short of their potential. Implementing changes in society thus comes hand in hand with the education of its members. By raising awareness and fostering interest we can create a thirst for knowledge and aim for education to take its most interesting and effective form: “learning by doing”.
In light of this perspective, we made the decision to carry out numerous initiatives meant to educate the public about synthetic biology and the issues it can help resolve, including this our problem: cadmium intoxication.
If education needs to be implemented for every member of society, we nevertheless decided this year to focus on, but not restrict ourselves to educating younger students. By educating young people, we hope to spark their interest in synthetic biology and inspire them to use engineering to make the changes we want to see rather than be a spectator to them.
Our initiatives were and carried out throughout our entire project. To reach the most people in the most effective way possible we decided to implement both initiatives with direct contact with the intended public and initiatives accessible to a broader public through intermediaries.
## BSGV (Biologie Synthétique par les Gymnases Vaudois = Synthetic biology by Vaudois High school)
In line with our commitment to promote hands-on education and foster a culture of "learning by doing," we have initiated the development of BSGV, a "mini-iGEM" competition designed specifically for high school students in our region.
The primary objectives of this endeavor are as follows:
- Cultivate an Engineering Mindset: Our foremost goal is to instil an engineering way of thinking in high school students before they embark on higher education. By engaging in synthetic biology projects, they gain early exposure to the problem-solving and innovation processes integral to engineering disciplines.
- Application of Synthetic Biology Knowledge: We aim to encourage students to learn and apply their acquired knowledge of synthetic biology. Participants are required to develop their own projects and articulate their ideas in written form, which deepens their understanding of the subject matter.
- Effective Communication Skills: To prepare students for potential careers in engineering, we challenge them to create posters and videos summarizing their projects. This not only helps them convey complex ideas effectively but also serves as a valuable skill set if they choose to pursue engineering studies.
To support students throughout the 6-week project duration, we provided multiple forms of assistance:
- Educational Videos: We created and recorded a set of educational videos covering synthetic biology and related subjects. These videos are accessible to all on the BSGV YouTube channel. The topics include introductions to synthetic biology, engineering principles in this field, the central dogma of biology, DNA, RNA, proteins, organisms used in synthetic biology, tools employed, safety measures, recent advancements, and real-world examples from iGEM projects. The videos were shared with the students to educate them on the basics of synthetic biology which in turn will help the development of their project.
- Online Guidance and Support: Our team members were available to answer any questions the students had during the entire project duration. This online support ensured that participants had the resources and guidance they needed to succeed.
The competition is set to conclude at the end of November with an award ceremony scheduled to take place at EPFL. During this event, each project will be presented, and medals and prizes will be distributed to the participating students. Moreover, students will receive constructive feedback on their work, both through in-person discussions with the team members and in written form, providing a more profound analysis and insights into their project and the methods they employed.
Our ultimate vision is to establish BSGV as an annual event that attracts a growing number of students. We have already garnered interest from multiple teachers and the DGEP (direction générale de l’enseignement post-obligatoire), suggesting a promising potential for future expansion and development. We are actively exploring opportunities to enhance and refine the program, to further foster students' passion for synthetic biology and engineering education.
By continuing to organize the competition, our aim is to inspire the next generation of engineers and synthetic biologists. We also encourage other teams to focus on educational initiatives to help achieve this broader goal.
More informations, as well as as our video courses and all of our competition's documentation is available to everyone on our dedicated BSGV page.

## BioQuest
In the realm of education, face-to-face interactions and hands-on experiences undeniably enhance the learning process, which is why our first initiatives were of this nature. However, it is important to recognize that these methods limit access to education to only a fraction of the population.
To address this limitation, we have decided to incorporate remote educational tools into our approach. The first outcome of this initiative is "BioQuest."
Synthetic biology is a complex subject with educational initiative often being reserved for high school and beyond in educational programs. With BioQuest, our aim is to bridge this educational gap by making synthetic biology accessible to learners of all ages.
BioQuest is an interactive game designed to introduce the fundamental principles of synthetic biology and delve into the world of some iGEM projects. This game comprises two main components: the main game and mini games.
The main game revolves around the exploration of key concepts in synthetic biology, incorporated into the narrative of our project. Each level introduces a new concept, gradually building the player's understanding. As the game progresses, players are challenged to combine these concepts to overcome higher levels of difficulty. Furthermore, in collaboration with other teams, we had the opportunity to expand the game by introducing new levels based on novel concepts and other iGEM projects.
Complementing the main game, BioQuest offers a selection of mini-games. These short, timed experiences enable players to delve into essential laboratory practices, such as laboratory techniques and lab safety. It provides an engaging hands-on experience within a virtual environment.
BioQuest stands as an educational endeavor that strives to inform individuals of all ages about synthetic biology while simultaneously entertaining them. Our goal is to break down barriers to learning and foster a deeper understanding of this complex field, ensuring that it is accessible to a wider and more diverse audience.
See more and access the game in our game page. We encourage you to take a look at the game page before downloading the game.
For more convenience, access the game here in IGEM software Gitlab repository.

## Wiki
Synthetic biology can be a complex subject, especially for those who aren't already familiar with the specific terminology used in biology. Understanding the basics of synthetic biology often involves learning specialized words and phrases that can be challenging for newcomers.
To address this challenge and make synthetic biology more approachable, we have developed a user-friendly version of our wiki, accompanied by an interactive dictionary that explains the specialized terms commonly used in this field.
This initiative aims to broaden the accessibility of our project, making it available to a wider audience. This audience includes high school and middle school students who may be just beginning to explore biology, as well as adults who have chosen career paths outside the realm of biology.

## Events
### Open days-booth
In the spring of 2023, EPFL (École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne) welcomed more than 25,000 visitors over a span of two days. During this occasion, our team had the opportunity to host a booth, which enabled us to showcase the iGEM competition, synthetic biology, and our own project. This endeavor allowed us to engage with a variety of people of every age, ranging from primary school children to university students and beyond.
To ensure that we could answer to the diversity of our public, we developed different activities to keep them engaged regardless of their age. For the youngest attendees, we created small games to convey the fundamental concepts of our project and synthetic biology. Through these activities, we sought to develop an interest in science in this younger audience following the spirit of the EPFL open days. For older students and adults, we had the opportunity to delve deeper into synthetic biology as well as engage in discussion with them, sharing our project’s objective, and methodologies. These interactions were intended to educate the broader public on synthetic biology and encourage other EPFL students to consider participating in iGEM in the future.
Our participation in this event not only allowed us to share our passion for synthetic biology but also served as a platform to promote the iGEM competition and its educational opportunities. We aimed to create awareness about the significance of hands-on learning and research in the field of synthetic biology and inspire a new generation of scientists and engineers.
The positive response from the visitors to this weekend of discovery reaffirmed the importance of science outreach and education. It showcased the value of making complex concepts accessible to any audience, sparking interest, and potentially shaping the future of STEM education and research.
### Gymnase visit
In the month of June, we had the pleasure of hosting two separate groups, each consisting of 12 students, from nearby high schools. We initiated the meeting with a presentation which introduced the fundamental concepts of synthetic biology and provided an overview of our own project. To gauge their comprehension and reinforce their learning, we provided a brief quiz to the students following the presentation. This served as an opportunity for them to consolidate their understanding and test their knowledge. The feedback from the students was positive, and they expressed their appreciation for this interactive learning experience.
We then started an open discussion with the students. This segment allowed them to ask any questions that they might have had during the presentation, seek further clarification on our project, and ask about our own academic experience. The students were keen to learn about the individual journeys that led our team members to become involved in synthetic biology. We were more than happy to share our personal experiences, insights, and motivations, hoping to encourage them to consider furthering their studies in engineering if not in synthetic biology.

### MINTeressiert die Welt!
Each year, the "MINTeressiert die Welt!" (MINTerest the World!) summer school program takes place at EPFL, with the goal of developing students' interest in a wide range of applications in the MINT field, which encompasses Mathematics, Computer Science, Natural Sciences, and Technology.
During this enriching summer program, our team had the privilege of representing the Life Sciences faculty and engaging with a group of 16 high school students from the Swiss-German region. Our primary objective was to introduce them to the world of synthetic biology.
Our interaction with the students began with an introductory session in which we provided an overview of iGEM and the fundamental concepts of synthetic biology. We also took the opportunity to present our own project, focusing on its objectives and significance.
To spark their interest and provide a hands-on experience, we delved into the protocols of a couple of synthetic biology experiments. The aim was to give the students a taste of the practical aspects of this field. Specifically, we explained the procedures involved in two experiments: DNA extraction from fruits and the expression of GFP (Green Fluorescent Protein) in Freeze-Dried Cell-Free systems.
Subsequently, the students were given the opportunity to apply these experiments. This hands-on approach allowed them to not only witness but also engage in the scientific process.
Throughout the event, we encouraged an open dialogue, and the students were encouraged to ask questions about the experiments, synthetic biology in general, or any related topics. By offering insights and addressing their inquiries, we aimed to deepen their understanding and inspire a lasting interest in the field of synthetic biology.

## Helping and encouraging the future of iGEM
We had the chance to exchange with a teacher and his students from a local high school that wishes to participate in iGEM in 2024. We gladly answered any question that they may have about the competition and shared with them some of our educational initiatives, such as our video courses on synthetic biology to give them the beginning of an idea of what iGEM entails. They greatly appreciated the videos. On top of the videos, they also had the opportunity to try out our video game: BioQuest in exchange for their feeback which was positive.
Our aim was to boost their enthusiasm and motivation for participating in iGEM 2024 through these interactions. We also hope that our initiatives, like BioQuest and our educational programs such as BSGV, will inspire other high schools to follow in their footsteps and get involved in the exciting world of synthetic biology and iGEM.
