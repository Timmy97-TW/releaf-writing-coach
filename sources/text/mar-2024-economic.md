We sought to understand the motivations of the industry concerning transport routes, the EU's Supply Chain Act, and deforestation-free raw materials. The EU's Supply Chain Act for deforestation-free products requires proof of the origin and entire supply chain, while the Human Rights Supply Chain Act, still being developed at the EU level, mandates human rights due diligence. However, many local stakeholders are unaware of these requirements due to poor local networks. There is a need to raise awareness and provide support in navigating the legal processes to obtain the necessary proof of compliance, particularly given the often unclear land-use rights in these regions. Raw material resilience is also a key issue. Europe currently relies 100% on imported rubber, which poses significant challenges for companies and drives the demand for alternative sources.
In our interview with Dr. Carsten Venz, Site Manager of the Taraxagum Lab at Continental (Anklam, Germany), we discussed Continental's motivation for investing in dandelion rubber. Their interest lies in the unique properties of dandelion rubber and the need to diversify rubber sources. We also reached out to Dr. Katharina Herzog, Project Manager at Continental, and asked her about the growing environmental pressures and political decisions requiring a reduction in the CO2 footprint of products.
Additionally, we met with Dr. Veronika Beer, Head of the Sustainable Development department of the Association of the German Rubber Industry (wdk). She highlighted the economic interest in natural rubber, particularly for products that must withstand high impact, and the ongoing necessity for sustainable alternatives in the industry.
Economic stakeholders play a crucial role in providing the capital needed to support research, development, and the scaling of new agricultural technologies. Their financial backing determines project feasibility and scope, shapes market demand, and facilitates supply chain integration. In our investigation into dandelion as an alternative rubber source, we recognized the importance of understanding market needs and the challenges faced by the rubber industry.
We discovered that various sectors are actively seeking alternative rubber sources due to pressures on rubber trees from climate change and leaf blight disease, as well as political demands for reduced environmental impact and improved labor protections. This economic shift also reflects a growing awareness of social consequences, which we addressed in discussions with stakeholders from this field.
In addition to these economic challenges, we explored the legal framework and political dimension that influence the implementation of new crops. Engaging with experts allowed us to uncover the complexities involved in implementation of dandelion cultivation, ensuring our approach incorporates perspectives from these critical areas.
